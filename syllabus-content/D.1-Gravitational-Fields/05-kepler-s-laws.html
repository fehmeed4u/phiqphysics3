1 LESSON 4.1
Projectile Motion
Topic Code: PHY101 | Level: A-Level
HOOK & DIAGNOSTIC
Figure 1: A ball launched at an angle follows a parabolic path.
Think about it: If you throw a ball at 30° above horizontal, what shape is its path? Why does it not continue in a straight line?
CORE KNOWLEDGE

Independence of Horizontal and Vertical Motion

Projectile motion can be analysed by considering the horizontal and vertical components separately. The horizontal motion has constant velocity (no acceleration, ignoring air resistance), while the vertical motion has constant acceleration due to gravity.

Key Equations $$v_{x} = u \cos\theta$$
$$v_{y} = u \sin\theta - g t$$
$$x = u \cos\theta \cdot t$$
$$y = u \sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2} g t^{2}$$
Figure 2: Parabolic trajectory showing velocity components.

Time of Flight & Range

The total time the projectile stays in the air, \(T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\). The horizontal range, \(R = \frac{u^{2}\sin 2\theta}{g}\). Maximum height, \(H = \frac{u^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{2g}\).

WORKED EXAMPLE

Projectile launched at 60°

1
Identify – A ball is thrown with initial velocity \(u = 25\,\text{m/s}\) at an angle \(\theta = 60^\circ\) from ground level. Find the range \(R\).
2
Data – \(u = 25\), \(\theta = 60^\circ\), \(g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2\).
3
Equation – Use \(R = \frac{u^{2}\sin 2\theta}{g}\).
4
Answer – \(R = \frac{25^{2}\sin 120^\circ}{9.81} = \frac{625 \times 0.8660}{9.81} \approx 55.2\,\text{m}\).
REAL-WORLD & HL EXTENSION

Real‑World: Sports

Figure 3: Basketball free‑throw trajectory.

In basketball, players adjust launch angle and speed to maximise chance of scoring. Optimal angle is near 45° for maximum range, but height of release changes the best angle.

HL Extension: Air Resistance

With drag, the path is no longer a perfect parabola. Range decreases and the descent becomes steeper. The equations become differential and require numerical methods.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
MisconceptionCorrection
Horizontal motion stops at the top.Horizontal velocity remains constant (if no air resistance).
Vertical velocity is zero at launch.Only at the apex; at launch it is \(u\sin\theta\).
Heavier objects fall faster.Without air resistance, all objects fall at the same rate (g independent of mass).
Warning: Many students forget that the vertical acceleration is constant \((-g)\) throughout the flight. The sign matters.

EXAM‑STYLE QUESTIONS
EXAM QUESTION 1
A projectile is launched from ground at 30° with speed 20 m/s. Determine the maximum height reached. Use \(g = 10\,\text{m/s}^2\).
\(H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} = \frac{400 \times 0.25}{20} = 5\,\text{m}\).
EXAM QUESTION 2
Explain why the horizontal range is maximum for a launch angle of 45° (in the absence of air resistance).
\(R = \frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}\). \(\sin 2\theta\) is maximum (=1) when \(2\theta = 90^\circ\), i.e. \(\theta = 45^\circ\).
EXAM QUESTION 3
A cannon fires a shell at 40° with speed 100 m/s. Find the time of flight and the horizontal range. (\(g=9.81\))
\(T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} = \frac{2\times 100 \times 0.643}{9.81} \approx 13.1\,\text{s}\). \(R = u\cos\theta \cdot T = 100 \times 0.766 \times 13.1 \approx 1003\,\text{m}\).
