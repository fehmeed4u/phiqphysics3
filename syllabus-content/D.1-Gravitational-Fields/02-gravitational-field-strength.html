Gravitational Field Strength
Why does a 1 kg rock weigh 9.8 N on Earth but only 1.6 N on the Moon? Learn how to predict gravity anywhere in the universe.

🎯 Hook – Moon vs Earth gravity

Watch an astronaut on the Moon: they can jump six times higher than on Earth with the same effort. The gravitational field strength g on the Moon is only about 1.6 N/kg, compared to 9.8 N/kg on Earth. This means every kilogram of mass experiences a much smaller pull.

Fig 1 — Same astronaut, different worlds: field strength determines weight.
💡 Diagnostic question (think – then click) — If you travel to a planet with twice Earth's mass and the same radius, your weight would be: twice as much (because g = GM/R²).

📐 Definition of gravitational field strength

Gravitational field strength g at a point is the force per unit mass experienced by a small point mass placed at that point. It is a vector quantity – direction is towards the mass creating the field.

Definition $$g = \frac{F}{m}$$

Units: N kg⁻¹ (equivalent to m s⁻²). The test mass must be small so it does not disturb the original field.

Fig 2 — A small test mass m feels force F = mg; g depends only on M and r.

📐 Field from a large mass – inverse‑square law

Combine Newton's law of gravitation \(F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}\) with \(g = F/m\) to get the field due to a point mass or spherical mass M at distance r from its centre:

Gravitational field strength from a mass M $$g = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

Notice that the small mass m cancels – so all objects fall with the same acceleration in a given field. The field follows an inverse‑square law: doubling r reduces g to one‑quarter.

Fig 3 — Radial field lines point toward the centre. Lines closer together = stronger field.

✍️ Worked example – Mars surface gravity & height

1
Identify: Use g = GM/r². For surface r = R; for height h, r = R + h.
2
Data: M = 6.39×10²³ kg, R = 3.39×10⁶ m, G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².
3
Equation (surface): g_s = GM/R² = (6.67e-11 × 6.39e23) / (3.39e6)² = 4.26e13 / 1.15e13 = 3.70 N/kg.
For g = 1.0 N/kg: r = √(GM/g) = √(4.26e13 / 1.0) = 6.53×10⁶ m. Then h = r − R = 3.14×10⁶ m.
4
Answer: Surface g = 3.70 N/kg. Height for g = 1.0 N/kg is 3.14×10⁶ m (≈3140 km above surface).
Fig 6 — Distance from centre r = R + h. Field strength drops as 1/r².

🌍 Real‑world: GRACE satellites & gravity mapping

NASA's GRACE mission measures tiny variations in Earth's g caused by changing water aquifers, ice sheets, and ocean currents. By mapping g, scientists monitor groundwater depletion and sea‑level rise.

✓ Examiner tip: In data‑analysis questions, a linearised plot (g vs 1/r²) gives a straight line; use its gradient = G·M to find the planet's mass.

🚀 HL Extension – Field inside a uniform sphere

For r < R, only the mass inside radius r contributes: \(g(r) = \frac{G M_{\text{inside}}}{r^2} = \frac{G \rho \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3}{r^2} = \frac{4}{3}\pi G \rho r\). Thus g increases linearly from 0 at centre to surface value.

Challenge: Earth's average density ρ = 5510 kg/m³. Predict g at half the radius. (Answer: g ≈ 4.9 N/kg, half the surface value.)

Exam question · Calculate
The Moon has mass 7.35×10²² kg and radius 1.74×10⁶ m. (a) Calculate the gravitational field strength on the Moon's surface. (b) Determine the weight of a 70 kg astronaut on the Moon. [4 marks]
(a) g = GM/R² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 7.35×10²²) / (1.74×10⁶)² = 4.90×10¹² / 3.03×10¹² = 1.62 N/kg ✓
(b) W = mg = 70 × 1.62 = 113 N ✓
Exam question · Graph analysis
A student plots a graph of g (N/kg) against 1/r² (m⁻²) for a planet and obtains a straight line with gradient 5.0×10¹². Calculate the mass of the planet. [3 marks]
✓ Gradient = G·M ✓ M = gradient / G = (5.0×10¹²) / (6.67×10⁻¹¹) ✓ M = 7.5×10²² kg
