Single‑Slit Diffraction
How light spreads after passing through a narrow opening – interference of Huygens wavelets

🎵 Everyday phenomenon – sound & radio waves

Stand near a tall building and listen to a street musician around the corner – you hear the music clearly because sound waves diffract (spread) around obstacles. Long‑wave radio signals reach valleys behind mountains, but short‑wave TV signals cannot – diffraction is strongest when the obstacle/gap size is comparable to the wavelength.

Fig 1 – Water waves fan out into a semicircle after passing through a narrow gap. The narrower the gap, the stronger the diffraction.

Foundation – Interference & Huygens

📐 Key definitions

Coherence: two sources emit waves with same frequency and constant phase difference.
Path difference: difference in distance from a point to two sources.
Constructive interference: path difference = nλ → waves in phase → bright fringe.
Destructive interference: path difference = (n+½)λ → waves out of phase → dark fringe.

💡 The principle of superposition: resultant displacement = vector sum of individual displacements.
Fig 2 – Two‑source interference in a ripple tank. Nodal lines (dark) occur where path difference = (n+½)λ.
Fig 3 – Huygens’ principle: every point across the slit acts as a wavelet source. Their superposition creates the diffraction pattern.

🔬 Introducing single‑slit diffraction

When light passes through a very narrow slit, it does not travel in a straight line but spreads out. According to Huygens, every point across the slit emits wavelets that interfere. The result is a pattern of a very bright central maximum flanked by dimmer secondary maxima, separated by completely dark minima.

Fig 4 – Actual laser pattern: bright central band, weaker side lobes.
Fig 5 – Intensity vs position on screen. Central maximum is twice as wide as side lobes and >20× brighter than first side peak.

❓ Why minima occur – pairing wavelets

For the first dark fringe at angle θ₁, take wavelets from the top edge and the midpoint of the slit. If their path difference is λ/2, they cancel. Geometry: (b/2) sinθ₁ = λ/2 → sinθ₁ = λ/b. Extending to the whole slit gives the general condition for minima:

b sinθ = nλ  (n = 1, 2, 3, …)
Fig 6 – Deriving the first minimum: path difference between top and middle ray = λ/2.
✓ For small angles: θ ≈ λ/b (in radians). Central maximum angular width = 2λ/b.
The master formula

🔑 Single‑slit minima condition

\boxed{b \sin\theta_n = n\lambda} \quad (n = 1,2,3,\dots)

Linear width of central maximum on a screen: \( w = \frac{2\lambda D}{b} \) (for small angles).
Proportionalities: θ₁ ∝ λ and θ₁ ∝ 1/b. Narrower slit → wider pattern. Longer λ → wider pattern.

Step‑by‑step worked examples

📝 Worked Example 1 – Finding slit width (IB style)

1
Given: λ = 532 nm, D = 2.50 m, distance to first minimum s = 4.0 cm.
2
Small angle: θ ≈ s/D = 0.040 m / 2.50 m = 0.016 rad.
3
First minimum condition: b sinθ = λ → b ≈ λ/θ = 532×10⁻⁹ / 0.016 = 3.33×10⁻⁵ m = 0.033 mm.
4
Answer: slit width = 0.033 mm. (If screen moved to 1.25 m, central width halves to 4.0 cm.)

📝 Worked Example 2 – Effect of wavelength change

Same slit (0.033 mm) but λ changes to 650 nm. Find new first minimum distance at D=2.50 m.

1
θ₁ = λ/b = 650×10⁻⁹ / 3.3×10⁻⁵ = 0.0197 rad.
2
s = D·θ = 2.50 × 0.0197 = 0.0493 m = 4.93 cm.

Trap corner – common mistakes

❌ “Diffraction is separate from interference”

✅ Single‑slit diffraction is interference of many wavelets from the same slit.

❌ “Wider slit → wider pattern”

✅ Wider slit → narrower pattern because θ₁ = λ/b decreases.

❌ “b sinθ = nλ gives bright fringes”

✅ This equation gives dark fringes (minima). Bright maxima are approximately halfway between.

❌ “Changing screen distance changes angles”

✅ Angles depend only on b and λ; D only changes linear size on screen.

One‑page summary
b sinθ = nλ (minima)θ₁ ≈ λ/bw = 2λD/bI ∝ b²

Central maximum contains >90% of energy. First side peak ≈5% of central. Narrow slit → wide, dim pattern; long λ → wide pattern.
