Energy in SHM
Kinetic energy, potential energy, total energy, and their variation with displacement and time

Energy overview

In SHM, energy continuously interchanges between kinetic (KE) and potential (PE) forms, while the total mechanical energy remains constant (assuming no damping).

The velocity of an object in SHM varies with displacement as:

v² = ω²(A² − x²)   ⟹   vmax = ωA (at x = 0)

From this, kinetic energy is:

KE = ½mv² = ½mω²(A² − x²)

Potential energy (stored in whichever store provides the restoring force):

PE = ½mω²x²

Total energy (constant, independent of x):

Etotal = ½mω²A²
Fig 1 — Energy vs displacement for SHM. KE is maximum at x = 0 and zero at x = ±A. PE is the reverse. Total energy is constant.

Total energy E = ½mω²A² = ½kA² (for a spring). Total energy is proportional to A² — doubling the amplitude quadruples the energy.

Energy as a function of time

If x = A cos(ωt), then:

KE = ½mω²A² sin²(ωt)     PE = ½mω²A² cos²(ωt)

Both KE and PE oscillate at twice the frequency of the displacement — they complete two full cycles per period T. This is because energy depends on the square of displacement or velocity.

At every instant: KE + PE = ½mω²A² = constant.

Where does KE = PE?

Setting KE = PE:

½mω²(A² − x²) = ½mω²x²  ⟹  A² = 2x²  ⟹  x = ±A/√2

At x = ±A/√2, the energy is equally split. Each form carries exactly half the total: KE = PE = ¼mω²A².

KE = PE at x = A/√2 E ∝ A² E ∝ ω² KE + PE = constant

Exam-style questions
Exam question · Mathematical / Multi-step
A particle of mass 0.150 kg undergoes SHM with amplitude 0.080 m and angular frequency 5.0 rad s⁻¹.

(i) Calculate the total energy of the system. [2 marks]
(ii) Calculate the speed when displacement is 0.060 m. [2 marks]
(iii) Calculate the displacement at which KE = PE. [3 marks]
(i) E = ½mω²A² = ½ × 0.150 × 25 × 0.0064 = 0.012 J ✓✓

(ii) v = ω√(A²−x²) = 5.0 × √(0.0064 − 0.0036) = 5.0 × √0.0028 = 0.265 m s⁻¹ ✓✓

(iii) KE = PE ⟹ ½mω²(A²−x²) = ½mω²x²
A²−x² = x² ⟹ A² = 2x² ⟹ x = A/√2 = 0.080/√2 = 0.0566 m ≈ 0.057 m ✓✓✓
Exam question · Theory / Explain
Explain, with reference to energy, why the speed of a particle in SHM is greatest at the mean position and zero at the extremes. [4 marks]
At the extremes (x = ±A), all energy is PE (= ½mω²A²), so KE = 0 and speed = 0. ✓✓

At the mean position (x = 0), PE = 0 so all energy is KE (= ½mω²A²), giving maximum speed vmax = ωA. ✓✓

On the energy graph, the KE curve peaks at x = 0 while PE peaks at x = ±A; their sum is always constant.
Exam question · Proportional reasoning (Hard)
Two SHM systems A and B have the same mass m and the same total energy. System A has twice the amplitude of system B.

(i) Show that ωA = ωB/2. [2 marks]
(ii) Determine the ratio vA,max : vB,max. [2 marks]
(iii) What does this tell you about the relationship between ω and A for equal-energy systems? [1 mark]
(i) EA = EB: ½mωA²(2A)² = ½mωB²A²
4ωA² = ωB² ⟹ ωA = ωB/2 ✓✓

(ii) vA,max = ωA·2A = (ωB/2)·2A = ωBA = vB,max
Ratio = 1:1 ✓✓

(iii) For equal-energy systems, ωA = constant. Maximum speed is the same regardless of how energy is partitioned. ✓
