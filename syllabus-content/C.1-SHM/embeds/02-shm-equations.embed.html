SHM Equations: Displacement, Velocity & Acceleration
The sinusoidal solutions, phase relationships between x, v and a, and the velocity–displacement equation

Angular frequency ω

The constant ω (omega) links the defining equation a = −ω²x to the period and frequency of the motion. It tells us how rapidly the phase of the oscillation changes — one full cycle corresponds to a phase change of 2π radians.

ω = 2π / T = 2πf    (unit: rad s⁻¹)
💡 ω is not the same as frequency f. Angular frequency is measured in rad s⁻¹, while f is in Hz. They are related by ω = 2πf.

Displacement as a function of time

The solution to a = −ω²x is a sinusoidal function. The form depends on where the object starts at t = 0:

x = x₀ cos(ωt)    (starts at x = x₀ = A, i.e. extreme position)
x = x₀ sin(ωt)    (starts at x = 0, i.e. mean position)

In the IB syllabus, both forms are accepted. The key insight: displacement varies sinusoidally with time, with amplitude x₀ (= A) and angular frequency ω.

💡 The IB data booklet gives both x = x₀ sin(ωt) and x = x₀ cos(ωt). You should be comfortable using either depending on initial conditions.

Velocity as a function of time

Velocity is the time derivative of displacement. Starting from x = x₀ cos(ωt):

v = dx/dt = −ωx₀ sin(ωt)

And from x = x₀ sin(ωt):

v = dx/dt = ωx₀ cos(ωt)

The maximum speed occurs when the sine (or cosine) factor equals ±1:

v_max = ωx₀ = ωA
⚠️ Velocity leads or lags displacement by π/2 radians (a quarter cycle). When displacement is at its maximum (extreme), velocity is zero — and vice versa.

Acceleration as a function of time

Acceleration is the time derivative of velocity. Starting from v = −ωx₀ sin(ωt):

a = dv/dt = −ω²x₀ cos(ωt) = −ω²x

This confirms the defining equation a = −ω²x. The maximum acceleration magnitude is:

a_max = ω²x₀ = ω²A    (at the extremes, x = ±A)

Acceleration is in antiphase with displacement (π radians out of phase) — when displacement is maximum positive, acceleration is maximum negative.

Fig 1 — Displacement (x), velocity (v) and acceleration (a) plotted against time. Note how v leads x by π/2, and a is in antiphase (π) with x. When x is at a maximum, v = 0 and a is at its most negative.

Velocity as a function of displacement

A critical equation that bypasses time entirely. Using the identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1:

v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²)

This tells us the speed at any displacement x. Key deductions:

PositionxSpeed |v|
Mean position0ωx₀ (maximum)
Extremes±A0
HalfwayA/2ω√(3/4)·A ≈ 0.866 ωA
⚠️ The ± sign indicates the object passes through most positions twice per cycle — once going one way, once returning. The speed is the same, but velocity direction differs.
Fig 2 — The velocity–displacement relationship traces an ellipse. At x = 0 the speed is maximum (ωA); at x = ±A the speed is zero. The ± indicates two directions of travel through each point.

Worked Example — Finding v_max, a_max and speed at a given displacement

A particle oscillates in SHM with amplitude 0.040 m and frequency 3.0 Hz.

1
Find ω: ω = 2πf = 2π × 3.0 = 18.85 rad s⁻¹
2
Maximum speed: v_max = ωA = 18.85 × 0.040 = 0.754 m s⁻¹
3
Maximum acceleration: a_max = ω²A = (18.85)² × 0.040 = 14.2 m s⁻²
4
Speed at x = 0.030 m: v = ω√(A² − x²) = 18.85 × √(0.0016 − 0.0009) = 18.85 × 0.02646 = 0.499 m s⁻¹
✓ Always show your substitution step clearly — examiners award marks for correct substitution even if the final numerical answer is wrong.

Exam-style questions
Exam question · Calculate
A particle of mass 0.200 kg undergoes SHM with amplitude 0.060 m and period 0.80 s.

(i) Calculate the angular frequency ω. [1 mark]
(ii) Write an expression for the displacement as a function of time if the particle starts at x = +A at t = 0. [1 mark]
(iii) Calculate the maximum speed and the maximum acceleration. [2 marks]
(iv) Calculate the speed when the displacement is 0.040 m. [2 marks]
(i) ω = 2π/T = 2π/0.80 = 7.85 rad s⁻¹ ✓

(ii) x = 0.060 cos(7.85t) ✓ [accept x = 0.060 cos(2πt/0.80)]

(iii) v_max = ωA = 7.85 × 0.060 = 0.471 m s⁻¹ ✓
a_max = ω²A = (7.85)² × 0.060 = 3.70 m s⁻² ✓

(iv) v = ω√(A² − x²) = 7.85 × √(0.0036 − 0.0016) = 7.85 × √(0.0020) = 7.85 × 0.0447 = 0.351 m s⁻¹ ✓✓
Exam question · Describe
Sketch displacement–time and velocity–time graphs for the same SHM oscillation on the same time axis, where the particle starts from x = +A at t = 0. Describe the phase relationship between the two graphs. [4 marks]
The displacement graph is a cosine curve starting at x = +A at t = 0, crossing zero at T/4, reaching −A at T/2, crossing zero again at 3T/4, and returning to +A at T. ✓

The velocity graph is a negative sine curve starting at v = 0 at t = 0, reaching −v_max at T/4, returning to zero at T/2, reaching +v_max at 3T/4, and returning to zero at T. ✓

The velocity graph leads the displacement graph by π/2 radians (T/4 in time). ✓ When displacement is at a maximum or minimum, velocity is zero; when displacement passes through zero, velocity is at its maximum magnitude. ✓
Exam question · Deduce (Hard)
Two particles P and Q undergo SHM with the same amplitude A. The angular frequency of P is twice that of Q (ω_P = 2ω_Q).

(i) Find the ratio v_P,max : v_Q,max. [1 mark]
(ii) Find the ratio a_P,max : a_Q,max. [1 mark]
(iii) At what displacement does particle P have the same speed as the maximum speed of Q? [3 marks]
(i) v_max = ωA, so v_P,max / v_Q,max = 2ωA / ωA = 2 : 1 ✓

(ii) a_max = ω²A, so a_P,max / a_Q,max = (2ω)²A / ω²A = 4 : 1 ✓

(iii) We need v_P = v_Q,max:
2ω√(A² − x²) = ωA ✓
2√(A² − x²) = A
4(A² − x²) = A²
4A² − 4x² = A² ✓
3A² = 4x²
x = A√3/2 ≈ 0.866A ✓
