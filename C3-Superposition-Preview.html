Principle of Superposition
When two waves meet, they add together – then continue unchanged. This simple rule explains interference, standing waves, and even noise‑cancelling headphones.

🎯 Hook – The Slinky® surprise

Watch two pulses on a Slinky® approach each other. When they meet, the rope briefly shows a much larger bump – then each pulse continues as if nothing happened. They don't bounce or destroy each other; they simply add.

Fig B — Two identical pulses pass through each other. At the moment of overlap (middle), the resultant displacement is the sum of the two.
💡 Diagnostic question (think – then click) — What does the rope look like when a positive peak meets a negative trough of the same size? (Answer: flat line – perfect cancellation, but only at that instant.)

📐 The Principle of Superposition

When two or more waves overlap at the same point in space and time, the resultant displacement is the vector sum of the individual displacements.

sresultant = s1 + s2 + ... + sn

Constructive interference – waves 'in step' (phase difference 0°, 360°, …). Peaks align → AR = A1 + A2 (bigger wave).
Destructive interference – waves 'out of step' (phase difference 180°, 540°, …). Peak meets trough → AR = |A1 – A2| (smaller wave).

Fig A — Top: wave 1, middle: wave 2, bottom: resultant sum (superposition).
Δφ = 2π · (Δx / λ)    AR = 2A cos(Δφ/2) (for identical amplitudes)

✍️ Worked example – Two‑speaker interference

1
Identify: Path difference → interference type. Use v = fλ.
2
Data: f = 343 Hz, v = 343 m/s → λ = 1.00 m. Δx = |1.80 – 1.50| = 0.30 m.
3
Equation: Δx/λ = 0.30. Phase diff Δφ = 2π(0.30) = 0.6π rad. AR = 2A cos(0.3π) = 2A · 0.588 = 1.176A.
4
Answer: Partial interference; resultant amplitude 1.18× single source amplitude.

🎧 Real‑world: Noise‑cancelling headphones

External mic picks up ambient noise (wave 1). Electronics create an identical wave shifted by 180° (wave 2). At your ear: wave 1 + wave 2 = silence. Superposition at work.

✓ Examiner tip: Destruction doesn't mean energy disappears – energy is redistributed to antinodes.

⚠️ Trap corner – common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrect view
Waves destroy each other during destructive interference.Energy is redistributed – total energy conserved.
Path difference must be zero for constructive interference.Any integer multiple of λ works (0, λ, 2λ…).
π phase difference always gives total destructive interference.Only if amplitudes are equal. Otherwise AR = |A1–A2|.
⚠️ Most common slip: forgetting the 2π in Δφ = 2π(Δx/λ). Without 2π, phase difference is dimensionless – but radians are needed for cos(Δφ/2).

Exam‑style questions
Exam question · Explain
(a) State the principle of superposition of waves. [2 marks]
(b) Two identical loudspeakers are connected to the same signal generator. A student walks along a line parallel to the line joining the speakers and hears alternating loud and quiet sounds. Explain why this happens. [3 marks]
✓ (a) When two or more waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements.
✓ (b) The waves from the two speakers are coherent. At different positions, the path difference changes. ✓ Where path difference = nλ → constructive (loud). ✓ Where path difference = (n+½)λ → destructive (quiet).
Exam question · Calculation
Two sources produce water waves of wavelength 0.12 m. At a point P, the path difference from the sources is 0.18 m. (i) State whether constructive or destructive interference occurs. (ii) If each source has amplitude 4.0 cm, calculate the resultant amplitude at P. [4 marks]
✓ (i) Δx/λ = 0.18/0.12 = 1.5 = 1 + ½ → destructive interference.
✓ (ii) For destructive with equal amplitudes, AR = 0 cm. (If amplitudes were unequal, AR = |A1 – A2|.)
