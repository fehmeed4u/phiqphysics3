## Equations
E1 γ = 1/√(1−v²/c²). E2 x′ = γ(x−vt). E3 t′ = γ(t−vx/c²). E4 Δx′ = γ(Δx−vΔt). E5 Δt′ = γ(Δt−vΔx/c²). E6 u′ = (u−v)/(1−uv/c²). E7 c = 3.00×10⁸ m s⁻¹ (constant, all inertial frames). E8 1 ly = 9.46×10¹⁵ m (exactly 9 460 730 472 580 800 m).

## Teaching points
P1 First postulate: laws of physics identical/invariant in all inertial frames. P2 Implications: no unique "correct" frame; no absolute velocity detectable; universe has no stationary frame. P3 Second postulate: c constant in all inertial frames. P4 Implication: space and time must behave differently than everyday intuition suggests. P5 Time is not invariant in a relativistic universe. P6 Space and time are linked as spacetime, not independent (contra Newton). P7 Lorentz transforms require the two frames' origins to coincide at t=0. P8 γ always ≥1; ≈1 for v≪c (negligible relativistic effects daily); large near c. P9 Clock sync methods: (a) flash midway between two equidistant clocks; (b) flash at unequal distances L1, L2 — set clocks to L1/c, L2/c before starting. P10 An observer moving relative to synchronized clocks sees them as unsynchronized. P11 Relativity of simultaneity, full worked case: light flashed from train-carriage centre (Event 0) to both ends, reflected back (Events 1, 2), returns to origin (Event 3). Train-frame observer: all simultaneous. Ground-frame observer: Event 1 (rear) precedes Event 2 (front) because carriage moves into the rearward beam's path; but return Events 3 coincide again. P12 Simultaneity rule: same place+time ⇒ simultaneous for all frames; different places ⇒ frame-dependent simultaneity. P13 Michelson-Morley's null result ended the ether hypothesis, paving the way for special relativity. P14 Two 19th-century problems: (i) Maxwell's electromagnetism contradicted classical mechanics; (ii) no experiment detected direction-dependent light speed from Earth's motion. P15 Velocity addition equation reduces to Galilean u′=u−v when u,v≪c. P16 u′ must always be <c, however large u and v are (below c).

## Worked examples (every step reproduced)
W1 (unnumbered, pre-A5.4): γ for v=1.50×10⁸ m/s (0.50c) = 1.15; event (x=5000 m, t=2.0 s) in S → x′≈−3.4×10⁸ m, t′=2.3 s in S′ (v=0.50c); assumption: origins coincided at t=0.
W2 (A5.4): light-year defined, 1 ly=9.46×10¹⁵m; Alpha Proxima at 4.02×10¹⁶ m = 4.25 ly.
W3 (A5.5): rocket at (x=20 ly, t=30 y) in S; S′ moves at 0.50c (γ=1.15) → x′=5.8 ly, t′=23 y; rocket's speed in S′ = 0.25c.
W4 (A5.6): event (x=250 m, t=1.7×10⁻⁶ s); observer at 0.75c (γ=1.51) → x′=−200 m, t′=1.6×10⁻⁶ s.
W5 (A5.7): spacecraft at 0.6c (γ=1.25) passes station, clocks zeroed; event at x=3000 m, t(station)=1.67×10⁻⁵ s → t′(spacecraft)=1.34×10⁻⁵ s.
W6 (A5.8): two particles approach at 0.60c and −0.60c (external frame); each measures the other's speed = 0.88c (symmetric case).
W7 (A5.9): rocket 1 at 0.75c, rocket 2 (behind, same direction) at 0.95c (external frame); rocket 1's observer measures rocket 2 approaching at 0.69c.

## Exam-style questions (source Q8–Q27, renumbered Q4–Q17 continuing from Lesson 02's Q1–Q3; Q13–19 light-clock derivation grouped into one multi-part question)
Q4(src8) rocket 0.5c passes Earth heading to a star-explosion 20 ly away; rocket passes Earth 20 y before the light arrives; find x′,t′ of explosion in rocket frame. Ans: x′=23 ly, t′=−11.5 y.
Q5(src9) Milky Way diameter 100,000 ly (Earth frame); observer moving 0.2c away (same plane) measures diameter L=L₀/γ≈9.79×10⁴ ly.
Q6(src10) two explosions 800 y apart (Earth frame), 2000 ly apart in space; rocket approaching at 0.2c measures Δt′≈408 y.
Q7(src11) electron at 0.9c; lab measures x=2.6 m, t≈9.6×10⁻⁹ s; γ=2.294; electron's own frame: x′≈0, t′≈4.1×10⁻⁹ s.
Q8(src12) two observers at relative 0.8c; one measures Δx=4.2 m, Δt=2.4×10⁻⁸ s; other measures Δx′≈−2.60 m, Δt′≈2.13×10⁻⁸ s.
Q9(src13-19) light-clock (Rachel in rocket / Mateo watching, mirrors M1,M2 separated by d) guided derivation: (a) identify Rachel's vs Mateo's diagrams; (b) rocket moves vΔt sideways per Mateo; (c) naive Galilean speed of light per Mateo (vector sum, contradicts postulate 2); (d) Newtonian diagonal path length √(d²+(vΔt)²); (e) distance light travels per Mateo = cΔt; (f) Pythagoras + constancy of c ⇒ Δt=γΔt′ (full time-dilation derivation, completed in Lesson 04); (g) explain in terms of constant c why observers disagree on the time.
Q10(src20) rocket at 0.1c shines laser forward: (a) onboard observer measures c; (b) Earth observer also measures c (not 1.1c).
Q11(src21) two rockets approach Earth-observer at 0.7c each; mutual approach speed (each rocket's frame) = 0.94c.
Q12(src22) spaceship 0.35c, proton fired forward at 0.95c (ship frame); station observer measures proton at ≈0.976c.
Q13(src23) alpha-decay: alpha 0.7c one way, daughter nucleus 0.2c other way (lab); daughter's frame sees alpha at ≈0.789c.
Q14(src24) beta-decay: antineutrino 0.95c, electron 0.75c, same direction (lab); electron's frame sees antineutrino at ≈0.696c.
Q15(src25) LHC protons at 0.9990000c opposite directions; one proton's frame sees the other approaching at ≈0.9999995c.
Q16(src26) two light beams approach at closing speed 2c (lab); "photon's frame" is not physically valid (γ→∞), but the addition formula still returns c.
Q17(src27) space race: ships at 0.6c, 0.7c same direction (mark's frame); faster ship sees the other receding at ≈0.172c.
