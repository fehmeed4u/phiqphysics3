C.2.1 Wave Properties and Wave Speed03
Definition Learn this wording

Travelling waves are
oscillations that transfer
energy from one place to
another without
transferring matter.

Waves are generated by oscillating sources, and these oscillations travel away from the source. They can propagate through a medium (air, water) or through a vacuum — no particles at all — depending on the type of wave.

01 The three lengths

Displacement, wavelength and amplitude

Displacement x — the distance of a point on the wave from its equilibrium position. A vector: it can be positive or negative. Unit: metres (m).

Wavelength λ — the length of one complete oscillation, measured from the same point on two consecutive waves: two crests, or two troughs. Unit: metres (m).

Amplitude A — the maximum displacement of an oscillating wave from its equilibrium position (x = 0). Unit: metres (m).

The horizontal line of zero displacement is the equilibrium position.

Fig 2 — Amplitude is measured from the equilibrium position to a crest; wavelength from one crest to the next.

02 The two times

Period and frequency

Period T — the time taken for a complete oscillation to pass a fixed point. Unit: seconds (s).

Frequency f — the number of complete oscillations to pass a fixed point per second. Unit: hertz (Hz).

Frequency and period
f = 1 / T
⚠️ For a frequency in hertz, the period must be in seconds — never milliseconds.
Fig 3 — On a displacement–time graph, one full cycle spans the period T.

03 Putting them together

Wave speed and the wave equation

Wave speed v — the distance travelled by the wave per unit time. Unit: metres per second (m s⁻¹).

The wave equation
v = f λ
v = λ / T
💡 For a wave of constant speed: as λ increases, f decreases. As λ decreases, f increases.
Fig 4 — Both waves travel at the same speed, so the shorter wavelength carries the higher frequency.
v = fλv = λ/Tf = 1/Tλ ↑ ⇒ f ↓ (constant v)

WE 1 Solution

Worked example 1 — solution

1
Identify. (a) The amplitude is the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position (x = 0). (b) The period is the time taken for one complete oscillation.
2
Data. Convert the amplitude from cm to m and the period from ms to s: A = 0.1 m, T = 1 × 10⁻³ s.
3
Equation. f = 1/T = 1 / (1 × 10⁻³)
4
Answer. (a) A = 0.1 m. (b) f = 1000 Hz — one thousand complete oscillations pass a fixed point every second.
✓ Both conversions matter. Leaving the period in ms would give 1 Hz instead of 1000 Hz.

WE 2 Worked example

Wavelength from a period and a speed

A travelling wave has a period of 1.0 μs and travels at a velocity of 100 cm s⁻¹. Calculate the wavelength of the wave, in m.

1
Known quantities. T = 1.0 μs = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ s; v = 100 cm s⁻¹ = 1.0 m s⁻¹.
2
Frequency from the period. f = 1/T = 1 / (1 × 10⁻⁶) = 1 × 10⁶ Hz
3
Rearrange the wave equation. v = fλ, so λ = v / f
4
Substitute and answer. λ = 1.0 / (1.0 × 10⁶) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m
✓ Examiner tip: convert every quantity to SI units before substituting.

05 Context

Why bass reaches you first through a wall

🎧 Real-world

Sound in air travels at about 340 m s⁻¹ whatever its pitch, so v = fλ fixes the wavelength: a 40 Hz bass note is about 8.5 m long, while a 4 kHz cymbal is about 8.5 cm.

Long wavelengths pass around and through obstacles far more easily than short ones — which is why a neighbour's music arrives as bass.

✓ State the wave equation before substituting, and quote the unit. Marks are given for v = fλ even when the arithmetic slips.

🚀 HL extension — speed is set by the medium

Frequency is set by the source; speed is set by the medium. When a wave crosses into a new medium its frequency cannot change, so v = fλ forces the wavelength to change instead. This is the basis of refraction in C.3.

Challenge: a 500 Hz sound wave passes from air (340 m s⁻¹) into water (1500 m s⁻¹). What happens to its wavelength?
λ rises from 0.68 m to 3.0 m; the frequency stays at 500 Hz.

Also expect: explain questions

A loudspeaker emits 40 Hz then 4.0 kHz into the same room. The speed is fixed by the medium, so v = fλ applies with v constant: as f increases, λ decreases — from 340/40 = 8.5 m to 340/4000 = 0.085 m.

And the amplitude trap

Make the 40 Hz note louder and the wavelength is unchanged. Louder means greater amplitude only; amplitude does not appear in v = fλ, and neither the speed nor the frequency has changed.
