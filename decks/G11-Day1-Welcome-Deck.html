Grade 11 · Day 101
Before anything else

This is a step up. You have two years to make it.

IB Physics asks more of you than what came before — more algebra, more uncertainty, more explaining why rather than quoting what. The first few topic tests may not go the way you want. That is information, not a verdict.

The students who do well here are not the ones who found it easy in September. They are the ones who stayed on the timeline: work in on time, feedback acted on, reflection log actually written. Build that habit now, in Year 1, and Grade 12 — with the IA and the trials — is far easier to walk into.

Grade 11 · Day 103
Assessment

How you are assessed this year

1
Every topic closes the same way — a topic test in Paper 1 and Paper 2 style, then a feedback-and-reflection lesson where the script comes back and goes into your reflection log.
2
Two summative assessments per term are reported. The rest of the topic tests still count as evidence of where you are.
3
Nine practicals across the year. Each one is placed inside the topic it tests and feeds your IA skills log — uncertainty, repeats, graphing, evaluation.
4
End of Year Examinations in April — Papers 1A/1B and 2, a practical and data-analysis paper, and the extended paper for HL.
5
Thursdays are HL. HL students sit additional assessments on A.4, B.4, C.1 HL and A.5.

Grade 11 · Day 107
Internal Assessment

The IA block — 26 April to 16 May

Week 1 — Introduction. The Scientific Investigation: criteria and command terms, exemplar analysis, choosing a research question, variables and control, risk assessment, provisional methodology sign-off.

Week 2 — Data collection. Supervised lab time across all four periods. Raw-data tables with uncertainties, repeat trials, teacher checkpoint at the end of the week.

Week 3 — Write-up. Uncertainty processing and propagation, graphing and gradient analysis, conclusion and evaluation, referencing. Draft submitted Thursday 14 May.

Nothing about the IA is a surprise if you treat the nine practicals as rehearsal for it. That is what they are for.

Grade 11 · Day 109
What I expect from you this year

Four things — prepared, adaptable, reflective, kind

Prepared

Ready before the lesson starts, not scrambling during it.

Adaptable

Responds to feedback and setbacks by adjusting, not defending.

Reflective

Turns every result into a named lesson, not just a mark.

Kind

Looks out for classmates without being asked to.

Grade 11 · Day 110
Expectation 1 of 4

Prepared

1
Bring your data booklet, lab book and a charged calculator to every lesson.
2
Do the reading before the lesson it's set for, not after.
3
Read the practical method before you walk into the lab, not at the bench.
Grade 11 · Day 111
Expectation 2 of 4

Adaptable

1
When feedback changes your method, change the method — don't defend the old one.
2
If a practical doesn't work first time, change one variable and try again.
3
Adjust how you revise based on your own test results, not on the syllabus order.
Grade 11 · Day 112
Expectation 3 of 4

Reflective

1
Complete the reflection log after every topic test — not the day it's checked.
2
Turn each returned script into a named list of mistakes, not just a mark.
3
Re-attempt the exact question you got wrong, not a different one.
Grade 11 · Day 113
Expectation 4 of 4

Kind

1
Share notes with a classmate who missed a lesson.
2
In feedback discussions, comment on the physics, not the person.
3
Look after your lab partner — safety, equipment and their share of the data.
