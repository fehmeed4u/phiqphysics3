1. A particle undergoes SHM described by x = 0.05 cos(4πt). What is the angular frequency and period?

ANS
  • Aω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 4π s
  • Bω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 0.50 s
  • Cω = 2π rad s⁻¹, T = 1.0 s
  • Dω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 2.0 s

2. In SHM, when is the velocity zero?

ANS
  • AAt the mean position only
  • BWhen acceleration is zero
  • CAt the extreme positions (x = ±A)
  • DVelocity is never zero in SHM

3. The phase difference between velocity and acceleration in SHM is:

ANS
  • A0 (in phase)
  • Bπ/2 rad
  • Cπ rad
  • D2π rad

4. For a particle in SHM, which correctly gives the speed at x = A/2?

ANS
  • Av = ωA/2
  • Bv = ωA/4
  • Cv = ωA√3/2 ≈ 0.866 ωA
  • Dv = ωA√2/2 ≈ 0.707 ωA

5. A particle in SHM has x = 0.10 sin(5.0t). What is the maximum acceleration?

ANS
  • A0.50 m s⁻²
  • B2.5 m s⁻²
  • C5.0 m s⁻²
  • D25 m s⁻²

WRAP-UPMark scheme [6]

✓ Moving from the extreme towards equilibrium, the restoring force acts in the direction of motion

✓ so it does work on the mass, transferring stored elastic potential energy to kinetic energy

✓ at the equilibrium position the stored potential energy is zero, so all of the constant total energy is kinetic

✓ Etotal = ½kx₀², so the total energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude

✓ because the elastic potential energy stored at maximum displacement depends on x₀²

✓ so doubling the amplitude requires four times the energy input

Common zero-scoring answer: "the total energy is bigger because the amplitude is bigger" — restates the relationship with no chain of reasoning and no ½kx₀².
