C.1 · SHM Equations02
Starter · question 1 of 5

Defining SHM

Which statement defines simple harmonic motion?

A The velocity is proportional to the displacement and in the same direction
B The acceleration is constant and directed towards the equilibrium position
C The acceleration is proportional to the displacement and in the opposite direction
D The period is proportional to the amplitude of the oscillation
Fingers up on three

Answer — C

SHM is defined by a ∝ −x: the acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed back towards equilibrium, written a = −ω²x.

Why the others fail

ADescribes velocity, not acceleration — and gets the direction wrong too
BAcceleration is not constant — it changes with displacement
DPeriod does not depend on amplitude at all
C.1 · SHM Equations03
Starter · question 2 of 5

Mass and period

A mass on a spring oscillates with period T. The mass is replaced by one four times larger, on the same spring. The new period is

A T/2
B 2T
C 4T
D 16T
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

T = 2π√(m/k), so T ∝ √m. Four times the mass gives √4 = 2, so the period doubles.

Why the others fail

ATreats T as inversely proportional to m
CTreats T as directly proportional to m, skipping the square root
DSquares the mass ratio instead of taking its square root
C.1 · SHM Equations04
Starter · question 3 of 5

At maximum displacement

A particle in SHM is at maximum displacement. At that instant

A velocity and acceleration are both maximum
B velocity and acceleration are both zero
C velocity is maximum and acceleration is zero
D velocity is zero and acceleration is maximum
Fingers up on three

Answer — D

At ±x₀ the oscillator has momentarily stopped, so v = 0, but the restoring force — and therefore the acceleration — is greatest there.

Why the others fail

AVelocity cannot be maximum where the particle has just stopped to reverse
BConfuses zero velocity with zero acceleration — they occur at opposite points
CSwaps the two — this describes the equilibrium position instead
C.1 · SHM Equations05
Starter · question 4 of 5

Pendulum and spring on the Moon

A simple pendulum and a mass–spring system are both taken to the Moon. Compared with on Earth, their periods

A pendulum increases, mass–spring unchanged
B pendulum unchanged, mass–spring increases
C both increase
D both unchanged
Fingers up on three

Answer — A

The pendulum period depends on g, so a weaker field gives a longer period. The mass–spring period depends only on m and k, so it is unchanged.

Why the others fail

BSwaps which oscillator depends on g
CThe mass–spring period has no g in its equation, so it cannot change
DIgnores that T = 2π√(l/g) changes when g changes
C.1 · SHM Equations06
Starter · question 5 of 5

Phase of displacement and acceleration

The phase difference between the displacement and the acceleration of a particle in SHM is

A 0
B π/2 rad
C π rad
D 2π rad
Fingers up on three

Answer — C

Acceleration is always opposite in sign to displacement, so the two curves are exactly antiphase: π rad (180°).

Why the others fail

AWould mean a and x always have the same sign — the opposite of a = −ω²x
BThis is the phase difference between x and v, not x and a
DA full cycle apart is no phase difference at all

C.1 · SHM Equations12
Chunk 1 · equation

Angular frequency, again

Angular frequencyω = 2π/T = 2πf

ω tells us how rapidly the phase of the oscillation changes — one full cycle is a phase change of 2π radians. It is measured in rad s⁻¹, not Hz.

C.1 · SHM Equations13
Chunk 1 · equation

Two forms of displacement

Starts at the extreme (x = x₀ at t = 0)x = x₀ cos(ωt)
Starts at the mean position (x = 0 at t = 0)x = x₀ sin(ωt)

Displacement varies sinusoidally with time, with amplitude x₀ and angular frequency ω. The IB data booklet gives both forms — pick the one that matches the starting condition.

C.1 · SHM Equations14

Which form do I use?

x = x₀ at t = 0x = x₀ cos(ωt)
x = 0 at t = 0x = x₀ sin(ωt)

Both are accepted on the IB syllabus. Be comfortable using either, depending on the initial conditions stated in the question.

Angular frequency and period

A particle undergoes SHM described by x = 0.05 cos(4πt). What is the angular frequency and period?

A ω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 4π s
B ω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 0.50 s
C ω = 2π rad s⁻¹, T = 1.0 s
D ω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 2.0 s
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

ω = 4π, T = 2π/ω = 0.50 s

Why the others fail

ASets T equal to ω's numeric value directly, instead of using T = 2π/ω
CReads ω = 2π, halving the coefficient of t
DT = 2π/ω computed incorrectly, doubling the correct period
C.1 · SHM Equations17
Check 1 · question 2 of 4

Starting from the mean position

A particle undergoes SHM starting at its mean position (x = 0 at t = 0). Which equation correctly describes its displacement?

A x = x₀ cos(ωt)
B x = x₀ sin(ωt)
C x = x₀ tan(ωt)
D x = x₀ cos(ωt) + x₀
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

sin(0) = 0, so x₀sin(ωt) correctly gives x = 0 at t = 0 and then rises sinusoidally.

Why the others fail

Acos(0) = 1, so this gives x = x₀ at t = 0 — the extreme, not the mean position
Ctan is not one of the two accepted SHM solutions and is not bounded like a real oscillation
DShifts the whole graph up by x₀, so it no longer oscillates about x = 0
C.1 · SHM Equations18
Check 1 · question 3 of 4

Displacement at t = 0

For a particle with x = x₀ cos(ωt), the displacement at t = 0 is

A zero
B x₀
C −x₀
D ωx₀
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

cos(0) = 1, so x = x₀ cos(0) = x₀. This form always starts at the positive extreme.

Why the others fail

AThat's the starting displacement for the sine form, not the cosine form
CWould need cos(0) = −1, which is not true
DConfuses displacement with the maximum speed, vmax = ωx₀

Finding the period

A particle has x = 0.020 cos(10t). Its period is

A 0.10 s
B 0.63 s
C 1.6 s
D 0.31 s

Differentiating x(t) twice, and reading the phase relationships that come out of it.

C.1 · SHM Equations21
Chunk 2 · equation

Velocity as a function of time

From x = x₀cos(ωt): v = dx/dtv = −ωx₀ sin(ωt)
From x = x₀sin(ωt): v = dx/dtv = ωx₀ cos(ωt)

Velocity is the time derivative of displacement — the gradient of the x–t graph at every instant.

C.1 · SHM Equations22
Chunk 2 · equation

Maximum speed

Maximum speed, when sin or cos = ±1vmax = ωx₀

⚠️ Velocity leads or lags displacement by π/2 radians (a quarter cycle). When displacement is at its maximum, velocity is zero — and vice versa.

C.1 · SHM Equations23
Chunk 2 · equation

Acceleration as a function of time

From v = −ωx₀sin(ωt): a = dv/dta = −ω²x₀ cos(ωt) = −ω²x

✓ This confirms last lesson's defining equation, a = −ω²x — derived, not assumed.

C.1 · SHM Equations24
Chunk 2 · equation

Maximum acceleration

At the extremes, x = ±x₀amax = ω²x₀

Acceleration is in antiphase with displacement (π radians out of phase): when displacement is maximum positive, acceleration is maximum negative.

When is velocity zero?

In SHM, when is the velocity zero?

A At the mean position only
B When acceleration is zero
C At the extreme positions (x = ±x₀)
D Velocity is never zero in SHM
Fingers up on three

Answer — C

At x = ±x₀ the object momentarily stops before reversing direction. v = ω√(x₀² − x₀²) = 0.

Why the others fail

AThe opposite of the truth — velocity is maximum at the mean position
BAcceleration is zero at the mean position, a different point from where v = 0
DSHM always reverses direction, which requires v = 0 momentarily
C.1 · SHM Equations28
Check 2 · question 2 of 4

Phase of velocity and acceleration

The phase difference between velocity and acceleration in SHM is:

A 0 (in phase)
B π/2 rad (quarter cycle)
C π rad (antiphase)
D 2π rad (full cycle)
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

Velocity leads displacement by π/2, and acceleration leads velocity by another π/2 — so a leads v by π/2.

Why the others fail

AConfuses v and a with each being in phase — true only of displacement with itself
Cπ rad is the phase difference between displacement and acceleration, not v and a
DA full cycle apart is no phase lag at all

Velocity from the sine form

For x = x₀ sin(ωt), the velocity is

A v = ωx₀ sin(ωt)
B v = −ωx₀ sin(ωt)
C v = ωx₀ cos(ωt)
D v = −ωx₀ cos(ωt)

Answer — C

v = dx/dt of x₀sin(ωt) is ωx₀cos(ωt) — the derivative of sin is cos, with no sign change.

BThis is the velocity for the cosine form of x, not the sine form

C.1 · SHM Equations34
Chunk 3 · note

What does the ± mean?

⚠️ The ± sign indicates the object passes through most positions twice per cycle — once going one way, once returning.

The speed is the same both times; only the direction of the velocity differs.

C.1 · SHM Equations35
Chunk 3 · worked example

Finding vmax, amax and speed at a displacement

A particle oscillates in SHM with amplitude 0.040 m and frequency 3.0 Hz. Find its maximum speed, its maximum acceleration, and its speed when x = 0.030 m.

Identify

Find ω first, then apply vmax = ωx₀, amax = ω²x₀, and v = ω√(x₀² − x²) for the last part.

Data

x₀ = 0.040 m · f = 3.0 Hz · x = 0.030 m

C.1 · SHM Equations36
Chunk 3 · worked example

Solving for ω, vmax, amax and v

1
Find ω: ω = 2πf = 2π × 3.0 = 18.85 rad s⁻¹
2
Maximum speed: vmax = ωx₀ = 18.85 × 0.040 = 0.754 m s⁻¹
3
Maximum acceleration: amax = ω²x₀ = (18.85)² × 0.040 = 14.2 m s⁻²
4
Speed at x = 0.030 m: v = ω√(x₀² − x²) = 18.85 × √(0.0016 − 0.0009) = 0.499 m s⁻¹

✓ Always show your substitution step clearly — examiners award marks for correct substitution even if the final numerical answer is wrong.

Speed at half amplitude

For a particle in SHM, which correctly gives the speed at x = x₀/2?

A v = ωx₀/2
B v = ωx₀/4
C v = ωx₀√3/2 ≈ 0.866 ωx₀
D v = ωx₀√2/2 ≈ 0.707 ωx₀
Fingers up on three

Answer — C

v = ω√(x₀² − (x₀/2)²) = ω√(3x₀²/4) = ωx₀√3/2

Why the others fail

ATreats speed as falling linearly with x, not by the square-root relationship
BThe same linear error, over-divided
DThis is the value for x = x₀/√2, not x₀/2

C.1 · SHM Equations41
Check 3 · question 4 of 4

Same speed, twice a cycle

A particle in SHM passes through x = +0.020 m twice in every cycle — once moving right, once moving left. Its speed at those two instants is

A different, because velocity is a vector
B the same, since v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²) gives equal magnitude
C zero, since it's not at an extreme
D undefined without knowing the initial phase
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

The ± sign only flips the direction of travel. The magnitude, ω√(x₀² − x²), is fixed by x alone — so the speed is identical both times.

Why the others fail

AConfuses velocity (which does differ in direction) with speed (which does not)
CSpeed is only zero at the extremes, ±x₀ — not at every non-extreme point
DThe speed–displacement relationship needs only x, x₀ and ω — no phase information

Common mistakes

Trap corner

MisconceptionCorrect view
The same sin/cos form works whatever the starting conditionThe form depends on it: x = x₀cos(ωt) starts at the extreme, x = x₀sin(ωt) starts at the mean position
The ± in v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²) means two different speedsIt means two different directions at the same speed — the object passes through each point twice per cycle
Velocity is zero wherever acceleration is zeroThey are zero at opposite points: v = 0 at the extremes (where a is maximum), a = 0 at the mean position (where v is maximum)

C.1 · SHM Equations44
Exam question · Calculate

A full pass through the equations

A particle of mass 0.200 kg undergoes SHM with amplitude 0.060 m and period 0.80 s. (i) Calculate the angular frequency ω. (ii) Write an expression for the displacement as a function of time if the particle starts at x = +x₀ at t = 0. (iii) Calculate the maximum speed and the maximum acceleration. (iv) Calculate the speed when the displacement is 0.040 m.

[6 marks]

(i) 1 mark. (ii) 1 mark for the correct form and substitution. (iii) 2 marks — one for each maximum value. (iv) 2 marks — one for substitution, one for the final value.

C.1 · SHM Equations45
Mark scheme [6]

What each mark is for

✓ (i) ω = 2π/T = 2π/0.80 = 7.85 rad s⁻¹
✓ (ii) x = 0.060 cos(7.85t) [accept x = 0.060 cos(2πt/0.80)]
✓ (iii) vmax = ωx₀ = 7.85 × 0.060 = 0.471 m s⁻¹
✓ (iii) amax = ω²x₀ = (7.85)² × 0.060 = 3.70 m s⁻²
✓ (iv) v = ω√(x₀² − x²) = 7.85 × √(0.0036 − 0.0016) = 7.85 × 0.0447
✓ (iv) = 0.351 m s⁻¹

✓ Examiner tip: substitute before you simplify — a correct substitution earns the mark even when the final arithmetic slips.
