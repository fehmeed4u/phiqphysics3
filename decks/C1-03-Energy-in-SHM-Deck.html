C.1 · Energy in SHM02
Starter · question 1 of 5

Angular frequency and period

A particle undergoes SHM described by x = 0.05 cos(4πt). What is the angular frequency and period?

A ω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 4π s
B ω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 0.50 s
C ω = 2π rad s⁻¹, T = 1.0 s
D ω = 4π rad s⁻¹, T = 2.0 s
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

ω = 4π, T = 2π/ω = 2π/(4π) = 0.50 s

Why the others fail

ASets T equal to ω's numeric value directly, instead of using T = 2π/ω
CReads ω = 2π, halving the coefficient of t
DT = 2π/ω computed incorrectly, doubling the correct period
C.1 · Energy in SHM03
Starter · question 2 of 5

Velocity in SHM

In SHM, when is the velocity zero?

A At the mean position only
B When acceleration is zero
C At the extreme positions (x = ±A)
D Velocity is never zero in SHM
Fingers up on three

Answer — C

At x = ±A the object momentarily stops before reversing direction. v = ω√(A² − A²) = 0.

Why the others fail

AThe opposite of the truth — velocity is maximum at the mean position
BAcceleration is zero at the mean position, a different point from where v = 0
DSHM always reverses direction, which requires v = 0 momentarily
C.1 · Energy in SHM04
Starter · question 3 of 5

Phase of velocity and acceleration

The phase difference between velocity and acceleration in SHM is:

A 0 (in phase)
B π/2 rad (quarter cycle)
C π rad (antiphase)
D 2π rad (full cycle)
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

Velocity leads displacement by π/2, and acceleration leads velocity by another π/2 — so a leads v by π/2.

Why the others fail

AConfuses v and a with each being in phase — true only of displacement with itself
Cπ rad is the phase difference between displacement and acceleration, not v and a
DA full cycle apart is no phase lag at all

Maximum acceleration

A particle in SHM has x = 0.10 sin(5.0t). What is the maximum acceleration?

A 0.50 m s⁻²

C.1 · Energy in SHM09
Hook

The pendulum that never quite comes back

Fig 1 — A swinging pendulum. A and E are the extremes; C is the equilibrium position. One full cycle is A → C → E → C → A.

At A, zero kinetic energy, maximum change in potential energy. Release it: gravity does work, trading potential energy for kinetic energy through B to C.

At C, kinetic energy is maximum and the change in potential energy has fallen to zero. The process repeats every half time period, through D to E and back.

💡 Diagnostic — think, don't answer. Release a real pendulum from A. Will it reach exactly the same height at E?

C.1 · Energy in SHM12
Chunk 1 · concept

Storing potential energy

In practice, no. Frictional forces dissipate energy, so all the energies progressively decrease — the pendulum never quite returns to its starting height.

When an object is pushed or pulled away from equilibrium, against the restoring force, work is done and potential energy is stored: elastic for a spring, gravitational for a pendulum.

The rule for every mechanical oscillation: all mechanical oscillations involve a continuous exchange of energy between kinetic energy and some form of potential energy.

Energy at equilibrium

A mass oscillates with SHM. At the instant it passes through the equilibrium position,

A kinetic and potential energy are both maximum
B kinetic energy is maximum and potential energy is minimum
C potential energy is maximum and kinetic energy is minimum
D both energies are zero
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

At x = 0 the displacement, and so the stored potential energy, is zero, and the speed is greatest — all of the constant total energy is kinetic.

Why the others fail

AEnergy is constant, but both cannot be maximum at once — that would double-count the total
CDescribes the extremes, not the equilibrium position
DContradicts the maximum speed found at equilibrium
C.1 · Energy in SHM18
Check 1 · question 2 of 4

Energy at the extremes

At the extreme positions of an SHM oscillation,

A kinetic energy is maximum
B potential energy is maximum and kinetic energy is zero
C both energies are equal
D the acceleration is zero
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

At ±x₀ the mass is momentarily at rest (v = 0, so kinetic energy = 0) and displacement is greatest, so potential energy is at its maximum.

Why the others fail

ADescribes the equilibrium position, not the extremes
CThe two energies are only equal at x = x₀/√2, not at the extremes
DAcceleration is maximum at the extremes — confusing zero velocity with zero acceleration
C.1 · Energy in SHM19
Check 1 · question 3 of 4

Pendulum potential energy

The potential energy of a simple pendulum bob, of mass m, raised a height Δh above its equilibrium position, is given by

A ½kΔh²
B ½mΔh²
C mgΔh
D mgΔh²
Fingers up on three

Answer — C

For a pendulum the potential energy is gravitational: Ep = mgΔh, using the height risen from equilibrium.

Why the others fail

AThe spring formula, ½kx² — the wrong quantity (k) for a pendulum
BMissing g, and squaring the height incorrectly — dimensionally wrong
DSquares Δh, which does not belong in gravitational PE

A 0.30 J

Total energy

C.1 · Energy in SHM23
Chunk 2 · graph

Energy against time — no losses

Fig 2 — Energy changes during oscillation of perfect SHM: potential, kinetic and total. Note the total is constant.

Potential and kinetic energy curves are mirror images: each rises as the other falls. Their sum — the total energy — is a horizontal straight line.

Both energy curves complete two full cycles for every one oscillation of the mass, because it passes through equilibrium twice per cycle.

C.1 · Energy in SHM24
Chunk 2 · graph

Energy against time — damped

Fig 3 — Energy dissipation in a practical oscillator: the total energy decays and the oscillating energy peaks decay with it.

Energy dissipation from an oscillating system is called damping.

The total energy line is no longer horizontal — it decays, and the peaks of the kinetic and potential curves shrink with it.

C.1 · Energy in SHM25
Chunk 2 · graph

Energy against displacement

Fig 4 — Variation of the energies of a simple harmonic oscillator with displacement, between −x₀ and +x₀.

Potential energy is a parabola with its minimum at x = 0; kinetic energy is the inverted parabola. Total energy is a horizontal line at height ½kx₀².

⚠️ Read the axis before you answer. Against time, both curves are sinusoidal at twice the frequency of the motion. Against displacement, they are parabolas and there is no frequency at all.

A unchanged

An oscillator of period T is undamped. The period of the variation of its kinetic energy is

A 2T
B T/2

C.1 · Energy in SHM33
Chunk 3 · worked example C1.5

From maximum kinetic energy to spring constant

Fig 6 — All the energy is potential at x₀ = 1.9 cm and all kinetic at the equilibrium position, where v = 85 cm s⁻¹.

A mass has a maximum kinetic energy of 0.047 J. (a) If its maximum speed was 85 cm s⁻¹, determine its mass. (b) State its maximum potential energy. (c) Determine the spring constant if the amplitude was 1.9 cm.

Identify

Maximum kinetic energy occurs at equilibrium. In perfect SHM the total energy is constant, so the maximum potential energy equals that maximum kinetic energy.

Data

Ek,max = 0.047 J · v = 0.85 m s⁻¹ · x₀ = 1.9×10⁻² m

C.1 · Energy in SHM34
Chunk 3 · worked example C1.5

Solving for m, Ep,max and k

1
Mass: Ek = ½mv² → 0.047 = ½ × m × 0.85² → m = 0.13 kg
2
Spring constant: Ep,max = ½kx₀² → 0.047 = ½ × k × (1.9×10⁻²)²
3
Answer: (a) m = 0.13 kg. (b) Ep,max = 0.047 J — equal to the maximum kinetic energy, because total energy is constant. (c) k = 2.6 × 10² N m⁻¹.
C.1 · Energy in SHM35
Chunk 3 · real-world / HL

Damping in practice — and beyond the equation

Why dampers exist

A car's shock absorber deliberately dissipates the suspension's oscillation energy so the body settles within about one cycle instead of bouncing for many. Building dampers do the same for earthquake and wind oscillations; a door closer converts the door's kinetic energy into internal energy of a fluid.

✓ Examiner tip: "where has the energy gone" wants named destinations — internal (thermal) energy and sound — not the word "lost".

HL — energy as a function of x

Ek = ½mω²(x₀² − x²)

Exactly the downward parabola of Fig 4. Challenge: at what displacement is Ek = Ep? Answer: x = x₀/√2 ≈ 0.71x₀.

C.1 · Energy in SHM38
Check 3 · question 2 of 4

What damping does

Damping in an oscillating system

A dissipates energy, so the amplitude decreases
B destroys energy, so the total energy of the universe falls
C increases the frequency without changing the amplitude
D converts potential energy into kinetic energy only
Fingers up on three

Answer — A

Damping is dissipation of energy by resistive forces: the amplitude falls. Energy is transferred, not destroyed.

Why the others fail

BConfuses "dissipated" with "destroyed" — energy is transferred, total energy is conserved
CDamping's defining effect is on amplitude; frequency stays essentially unchanged
DDescribes the normal SHM exchange, not damping — damping removes energy from the oscillation

C.1 · Energy in SHM40
Check 3 · question 4 of 4

Acceleration at the extremes

At the extreme position of an SHM oscillation, the mass is momentarily at rest. At that instant its acceleration is

A zero, because the mass has stopped
B maximum, directed towards equilibrium
C maximum, directed away from equilibrium
D undefined
Fingers up on three

Answer — B

Velocity and kinetic energy are zero at the extremes, but potential energy, restoring force and acceleration are all maximum there — directed back towards equilibrium.

Why the others fail

AConfuses zero velocity with zero acceleration — the two are independent
CGets the direction backwards — the restoring force always points towards equilibrium
DAcceleration is well-defined and maximum at the extremes

C.1 · Energy in SHM45
Mark scheme [6]

What each mark is for

✓ Moving from the extreme towards equilibrium, the restoring force acts in the direction of motion
✓ so it does work on the mass, transferring stored elastic potential energy to kinetic energy
✓ at the equilibrium position the displacement, and so the stored potential energy, is zero, so all of the constant total energy is kinetic
✓ Etotal = ½kx₀², so the total energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude
✓ because the elastic potential energy stored at maximum displacement depends on x₀²
✓ so doubling the amplitude requires four times the energy input

✓ Examiner tip: "explain" wants the causal chain — force → work → energy transfer — not just the final numeric relationship.
